**Story in one line:** it rains, there are two umbrellas, and CHIEF shoves PIP aside to take the enormous
one. He then struts to the spot he considers best — directly under a **broken gutter** — and gloats for ten
seconds while it quietly fills his bowl-shaped canopy. Then he sweeps the umbrella down to point mockingly
at PIP, and empties the whole reservoir over his own head. PIP, bone dry, offers him the little umbrella.

**The seeds:** the **broken gutter** dripping above the doorway in frame 1 (with its splash mark on the
pavement showing exactly where it lands), and the **two umbrella shapes** — one a deep bowl, one small and flat.

## What's new in v7

**Fresh scenario and a first-time mechanic.** `SC8 Everyday Friction` is used for the first time, making
**NP1×SC8 a fresh pattern×scenario pair**, and `CM-C3 Instant Karma` is a first-time comedy mechanic.
Queue position sits correctly in the descending order: 9.2 → 9.0 → 8.9 → 8.8 → 8.7 → **8.6**.

**A different retention engine: dramatic irony.** v6 ran on *curiosity* (a balance scale the audience wanted
the total from). v7 runs on **knowing something the character doesn't** — the audience watches the canopy
fill for 21 seconds while CHIEF never looks up. That's a stronger hold than curiosity, because the viewer
isn't wondering *what* will happen, they're waiting for a specific guaranteed event and they don't want to
miss it. Different psychological driver, so the two episodes don't feel like the same trick.

**The silence beat is filled, for the first time in the series.** In v1–v6 the 11-second silence was
near-total. Here the music cuts but **the pour keeps running** — one relentless, indifferent sound of the
payoff being physically loaded. Far more unbearable than quiet.

**The pattern break needs no visual cut.** At 0:16 nothing changes on screen; the score is simply removed
and the pour becomes exposed. It's the first episode where the turn is achieved purely by subtraction.

**Signature-sound inversion.** v1 = stamp · v2 = booster · v3 = block clack · v4 = panel slam ·
v5 = discovery sting · v6 = sweet clatter. **v7 = water**, and it's the most literal escalation yet: four
distinct *physical states* — `drip → trickle → pour → SPLASH` — mapping exactly onto the four acts. The
inversion is the cleanest in the series: after the enormous splash, the final sound of the episode is **one
single drip off his nose**, the signature returned to its smallest possible form, exactly where it began.

**PIP's kindness is the payoff beat.** The Character Bible requires the Hero to be "vindicated *by the
twist*, never by their own retaliation." Here PIP tries to warn the man who shoved him, is waved off, and
still ends the video **offering him his umbrella.** No gloating, no *I told you so*. It's the most on-brand
beat in the series so far, and a strong Subscribe driver.

## The one invariant that cannot break
**CHIEF must stand inside the gutter's drip line in every frame from C3 to C7, and must never look up
before C7.** That's the entire mechanism — he loads the bucket himself, by choosing where to stand, and
fires it himself, by pointing. There is no external agent anywhere in this episode. It's written into the
compliance checklists in files 01, 02 and 04.

## Content-safety note
Rain and a soaking can drift into "distress" territory, so the guardrails are explicit: CHIEF is **wet and
embarrassed, nothing more** — no shivering, coughing, illness, lightning, storm menace, or slipping injury,
and the C1 shove must read as comic and soft rather than violent. Written into files 01, 02 and 04.
