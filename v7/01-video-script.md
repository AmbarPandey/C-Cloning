| Device | Where | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold-open hook** | 0:00–0:02 | Opens on the first rain hitting the pavement and a hand shoving PIP aside. Conflict in two seconds, no setup |
| **Dramatic irony** | 0:06–0:27 | **The audience can see the canopy filling; CHIEF cannot.** This is the retention engine — 21 seconds of *knowing something the character doesn't*, which is a stronger hold than curiosity because the viewer is waiting for a specific, guaranteed event |
| **The sag meter** | 0:06–0:27 | The canopy's droop is a visible, wordless load indicator. Every second it gets heavier and the payoff gets bigger |

| **Pattern interrupt** | 0:16 | Music cuts mid-phrase on his victory pose; only the pouring water remains |
| **The near-miss** | 0:22–0:27 | PIP tries to warn him and is silenced — one last twist of the knife that makes the audience lean in |
| **The twist** | 0:27–0:31 | His own pointing gesture dumps the reservoir |
| **Loop trigger** | 0:31–0:32 | Returns to the C1 composition so viewers rewatch to spot the gutter |

**Why this differs from v6's meter:** v6's balance scale was an *arithmetic* meter that CHIEF eventually
read himself. v7's sag is a *physical load* meter that CHIEF **never sees** — it is above his own head the
entire time. v6 ran on curiosity ("what's the total?"); v7 runs on dramatic irony ("he has no idea").
Different psychological driver, so the two don't feel like the same trick.

## Scene-by-scene script

> Legend — **VIS** visual/staging · **ACT** action · **CAM** camera · **VO** narration (optional layer) · **SFX** sound · **EMO** emotion

### C1 — HOOK + SEED (0:00–0:02)
- **VIS:** A `PAPER` shop front on an `ASPHALT` pavement. First `SKY`-blue raindrops striking the ground in flat splashes. Above the doorway: a **broken `ASPHALT` gutter with an `ALERT_RED` rust crack**, dripping a steady bead of water onto the pavement below (**SEED A** — the water source, and the flat splash marks show exactly where it lands). Beside the door, an umbrella stand holding two umbrellas: one **enormous `BRAND_YELLOW` umbrella with a deep bowl-shaped canopy**, and one **tiny flat `POP_TEAL` one** (**SEED B**). PIP is reaching for an umbrella.
- **ACT:** CHIEF struts in and **shoves PIP aside** with one oversized glove, eyes locked on the big one.
- **CAM:** Static wide establishing (this exact framing returns in C8).
- **VO:** *"It started to rain."*
- **SFX:** Light rainy comedy bed (low); first rain patter; **a single clear gutter drip**; a small "oof" as PIP is shoved.
- **EMO:** CHIEF smug · PIP worried.

### C2 — SETUP (0:02–0:06)
- **VIS:** Medium two-shot at the umbrella stand.
- **ACT:** CHIEF seizes the enormous umbrella and **snaps it open with a huge flourish** — the canopy is unmistakably a deep bowl. He gloats down at PIP. PIP, unbothered, quietly takes the tiny flat one and opens it.
- **CAM:** Slow push-in that ends framed on the deep bowl of the open canopy.
- **VO:** *"There were two umbrellas. He took the big one."*
- **SFX:** A big umbrella *fwoomp*; a small umbrella *pop*; a dismissive "pfft"; rain patter building. The gutter drip continues underneath.
- **EMO:** CHIEF gloating · PIP neutral.

### C3 — ESCALATION 1 (0:06–0:11)
- **VIS:** The rain thickens. CHIEF surveys the pavement and picks the spot he considers best — **directly beneath the broken gutter** — and plants himself there.
- **ACT:** He gloats across at PIP, twirling the huge umbrella. Above and behind his head, the gutter's drip becomes a **steady trickle running into his canopy.** He does not look up. PIP stands dry a few steps away.
- **CAM:** Static wide; a 4-frame hold as the trickle first enters the canopy.
- **VO:** *"Obviously."*
- **SFX:** Rain heavier; the drip becoming a **trickle** — a distinctly different, more continuous sound; a proud little fanfare.
- **EMO:** CHIEF gloating · PIP content.

### C4 — ESCALATION 2 (0:11–0:16)
- **VIS:** Rain heavier still. The gutter is now **pouring**. The huge canopy **visibly sags** — the fabric bowing downward, the pool inside deepening, the shaft beginning to lean under the weight.
- **ACT:** CHIEF gloats harder, gesturing at his magnificent umbrella and then at PIP's tiny one, chest out. Still never looks up. PIP remains perfectly dry, blinking placidly.
- **CAM:** Slight push-in; a 3-frame hold on each stage of the sag.
- **VO:** *"And he found the best spot to enjoy it from."*
- **SFX:** Gutter now a **pour**; a low fabric *creak* under load; rain at full intensity.
- **EMO:** CHIEF triumphant · PIP content.
- **Note:** this is the comeuppance loading. Every second he spends gloating makes the payoff physically larger — his own behaviour is filling the bucket.

### C5 — ANTICIPATION (0:16–0:22)
- **VIS:** CHIEF strikes an enormous triumphant pose — chest out, chin high, one glove on his hip — with the canopy hugely distended above him, straining, water visibly heavy inside it.
- **ACT:** Long proud held tableau. He believes he has won the rain.
- **CAM:** Low hero-angle push-in that settles into the hold, the bulging canopy dominating the top of frame.
- **VO:** *"Dry as a bone."* → then silence.
- **SFX:** Bed swells to a peak, then **CUTS TO SILENCE**; only the steady pouring of the gutter into the canopy remains — now the loudest thing in the video.
- **EMO:** CHIEF triumphant (L3).

### C6 — PATTERN BREAK (0:22–0:27)
- **VIS:** In near-silence, the canopy sags to its absolute limit, fabric taut, the shaft bending. PIP notices, steps forward and **raises a small hand to warn him**, opening his mouth.
- **ACT:** CHIEF, without even looking, raises one glove in a *don't-interrupt* gesture and keeps posing. PIP lowers his hand, glances at the canopy, and quietly takes one step back.
- **CAM:** Static two-shot, then a slow tilt up to the straining canopy.
- **VO:** (silence — let it breathe) optional whisper *"…he tried to say something."*
- **SFX:** Silence except the pour, a rising fabric *strain*, and one suspense *tick*. PIP's small step back is audible.
- **EMO:** CHIEF oblivious · PIP concerned.

### C7 — TWIST / INSTANT KARMA (0:27–0:31)
- **VIS:** CHIEF finally makes his big finishing move: he **sweeps the enormous umbrella down and forward to point mockingly at PIP** — and the entire accumulated reservoir cascades out of the tipping canopy **straight down over his own head.**
- **ACT:** He is instantly drenched — hair flattened, sash sodden, medals drooping, mustache limp, cap slumped over one eye. He blinks once. PIP, two steps away, is **completely dry.** His face snaps **triumphant → shocked → panicked.**
- **CAM:** Quick punch-in on the tipping canopy, then settle wide; ~0.5 s freeze on the drenched CHIEF beside bone-dry PIP.
- **VO:** *"Then he pointed."*
- **SFX:** Music SLAMS back; an enormous **SPLASH**; water hitting pavement; a single last *drip* off his nose; record-scratch.
- **EMO:** CHIEF panicked · PIP gleeful. **← the SHARE trigger.**

### C8 — PAYOFF / LOOP (0:31–0:32)
- **VIS:** Composition matches C1. Drenched CHIEF stands in a small puddle, the now-empty giant umbrella hanging limp and inverted in his glove. Beside him, PIP **holds out his tiny `POP_TEAL` umbrella** — offering it, kindly, no gloating.
- **ACT:** CHIEF hunches under the little umbrella, far too big for it, mortified. PIP turns and gives a small wave to camera. One last drip falls off the gutter above.
- **CAM:** Return to C1 framing; hard cut out ≤1 s after the wave.
- **VO:** *"Every time."* (series button; also seeds the loop)
- **SFX:** A last single gutter *drip*; warm resolve chord; button *ding*.
- **EMO:** CHIEF sheepish · PIP relieved. **Loop seam:** C8 reuses C1's exact framing, background plate and gutter position — only the rain state, the umbrellas and the cast's condition differ, so the replay re-reads the gutter and the canopy shape with the ending in mind.

> **PIP's kindness is deliberate and load-bearing.** The Character Bible requires the Hero/Underdog to be
> "vindicated *by the twist*, never by their own retaliation." PIP never gloats, never says *I told you so*,
> and ends the video **helping** the man who shoved him. That is what makes him worth subscribing to, and it
> is the single most on-brand beat in the series so far.
