## 3. The silence beat (0:16-0:27) - the single most important audio move

An **11-second musical silence**, cut **mid-phrase** on the frame the line reaches maximum tension during CHIEF's backswing.

**Only these sounds are permitted inside it:**

| Time | Sound | Level | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:16-0:22 | `SFX_line_vibration_v1` (continuous, thin) | **-16 dB** | The taut line humming under tension. A thin, high-pitched, ominous tone. The only sound before the snap |
| 0:16-0:27 | `SFX_water_lap_v1` (continuous) | -26 dB | The pond, pushed far back but never fully gone. The world continues |
| **0:22** | **`SFX_line_snap_v1`** | **-6 dB -- loudest sound in the silence** | **THE SNAP. Sharp, musical, clean. Like a guitar string breaking. Must be startling** |
| 0:22.5 | `SFX_rod_whip_v1` | -12 dB | The rod whipping forward bare -- a fast thin swoosh |
| **0:23** | **`SFX_splash_large_v1`** | **-8 dB** | **The gear cluster hitting the water BEHIND CHIEF. Large, heavy, multi-item. Obviously significant** |
| 0:23.5 | `SFX_ploop_tiny_v1` | **-18 dB** | **The empty hook landing in front. Pathetically tiny. The CONTRAST with the splash behind is the audio punchline** |
| 0:24-0:25 | (spreading ripples -- no new SFX, just the fading splash tail) | -- | Let the large splash ring out and die naturally |
| 0:25 | `SFX_bubble_pop_v1` | -16 dB | The red bobber surfacing briefly. One pop |
| 0:26 | (silence -- only the distant lapping) | -- | The moment of total emptiness. CHIEF has nothing left |

- **No VO** in this window except an optional whispered *"...snap."* at ~0:22.5, <=0.3 s.
- **No music, no stings.** The snap, the splash, and the ploop must own the space entirely.

> **The audio punchline of the silence is the CONTRAST between 0:23 and 0:23.5.** The massive splash
> behind (all his gear, all his money, all his showing off -- gone) followed immediately by the pathetic
> ploop in front (one empty hook, all he has left). Mix them exactly 10 dB apart (-8 vs -18) so the
> contrast is unmistakable. The ploop should sound like an apology.

## 5. Loudness / delivery (platform-ready)
- Integrated target **~-14 LUFS**, true-peak **<=-1 dBTP**.
- **Duck BGM ~4-6 dB under VO** for VO lines; release fully into the silence.
- VO peaks ~-12 to -10 dBFS, ~3 dB above the ducked bed.
- **The clip-on sounds are the rhythm section.** In C3-C4, the comedy bed's banjo plays one strum per
  attachment, making each lure/weight addition feel like a musical downbeat. When the music cuts at 0:16,
  the additions have already stopped (CHIEF is now casting). The silence is populated by a completely
  different sonic world: tension, snap, splash, nothing.
- **The signature sound of this episode is the SNAP (0:22).** It is sharp, clean, and musical -- it
  should sound like a guitar string breaking. The fishing line has been humming under tension (0:16-0:22),
  building the audience's anticipation of this exact moment, and then it arrives with complete finality.
  Nothing can undo it.
- **The secondary signature is the splash/ploop contrast (0:23/0:23.5).** These two sounds, 0.5 s apart
  and 10 dB apart in volume, ARE the audio punchline of the silence. The big splash = everything he
  invested flying away. The tiny ploop = all he has left. Mix them so the contrast is laughable.
- **The protected sounds** are the **0:01.5 stick pickup** (Seed A -- if it is inaudible, the twist has
  no fair-play), the **0:22 snap** (the loudest moment), and the **0:23.5 ploop** (the comic counterpoint
  to the splash -- if it is too loud it loses its patheticness; if too quiet it disappears).
- **The loudest moments, in order:** the **0:22 snap** (-6) -> the splash behind (0:23, -8) ->
  the bobber attach clonk (0:11.5, -9). The snap should feel sudden and irreversible.
- **Never compress the silence up.** 0:16-0:27 has no music; the line hum and the subsequent snap/splash
  should feel exposed and stark, not normalized to the same volume as the music sections.
