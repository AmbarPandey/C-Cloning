**Story:** a market stall on a sloped street. Every customer gets a crate of round fruit **and a chock**, and
the stall's pictogram says: chock it or it rolls. PIP chocks his and takes two apples. CHIEF wants a taller
stack, so he **pulls his chock out and props his crate up on it**. He's served first, hoists a full crate,
gets the tick — **he wins.** Then the crate tips off the prop it's standing on and the whole load rolls away
downhill. PIP's chocked crate is the only full one left on the street.

**Seeds:** the slope arrow + chock pictogram, and the chock he pulls at 0:04 — **one object asked to do two
jobs it cannot do at once.**

## What I carried over from v24/v25
- **Full confidence, proven nodes.** v24 was the only Confidence-1.00 episode in the last batch and scored highest. v27 is 1.00 with all edges 5/5.
- **A mechanism explainable in one sentence.** v24: harder push → faster spin → never clears the opening. v27: **chock moved → crate propped → stack raised → tips off the prop.**
- **One measurement on screen throughout.** v24 had the door's rotation; v25 the two tile tokens; v27 has **the slope itself**, visible in every frame with a kerb line and paving joints, so every object's stability is continuously readable.

## The craft point I'm most pleased with
**One object, two jobs.** The entire episode turns on a single wedge being asked to chock *and* prop
simultaneously. There is no extra part to explain, nothing off-screen, and the audience watches him create
the fault himself in one three-frame move at 0:04. The audio states it outright: `SFX_chock_slide_v1` at
0:03 is PIP seating his chock correctly, `SFX_chock_pull_v1` at 0:04 is CHIEF removing his — **the same
object, opposite sounds, matched levels, one second apart.** The right answer and the wrong answer handed
to the audience inside a single second.

And the signature closes the loop: the episode's first sound is a chock being seated correctly, and its last
is that same chock being seated again — by PIP, on CHIEF's behalf.
