**Story:** a revolving door with a direction arrow and a one-per-compartment rule. PIP steps in and waits.
CHIEF shoves into the next compartment, wedges his crate across it, and pushes harder and harder to get
round first. The jammed crate turns his compartment into a paddle: the faster he pushes, the further the
door carries him — a full extra revolution, straight back out onto the pavement. PIP, who pushed nothing,
is now inside.

**Seeds:** the direction arrow + one-person pictogram, and the crate he wedges at 0:04.

## What's new
- **Rotation — structurally new for the series.** Nothing in v1–v23 uses rotational motion. A turning door produces continuous visible change with no cutting required, and a revolving door **is** a loop, so the loop-seam requirement is satisfied by the premise rather than bolted on.
- **The score pays for the batch.** v18–v23 spent real points opening seven unused nodes (`NP5`, `NP7`, `NP8`, `TW6`, `TW7`, `TW9`, `TW10`) at scores of 6.0–8.1. v24 is the workhorse that funds them: full confidence, fresh pair, 8.8.
- **The music's tempo is tied to the door's rotation.** He doesn't just spin faster, he drags the score along — and when the music cuts at 0:16 the bearing carries on at the tempo the music was holding.
- **A countdown with no numbers.** The bearing loop carries a periodic per-wing thump, so during the silence the listener can *count the revolutions he's failing to get off at*. Each of the three crate creaks is aligned to a thump.

## The craft point I'm most pleased with
The contrast is doing all the work and it needs no explanation: **PIP expends zero effort and travels at
exactly the same speed.** Both compartments of the same door are in frame together for fourteen seconds, so
the viewer watches one character strain and the other stand still, arriving identically — until the strain
is precisely what carries him past his own exit.
